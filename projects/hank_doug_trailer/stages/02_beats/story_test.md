# Story test, stage 02 (from beats.json alone, no lines, no song)

## What a first-time viewer would say the film is about
A pencil-drawn goat on blank canvas opens her mouth and a ring of paint spreads out and colours the mountain. The
colour runs down to a marmot, who stands up and sends out two bigger rings, and the rings paint a road and a parked
red wagon where a dog with lifted ears sits beside a bear holding the reins. The wagon rolls out behind a horse, a
zebra, a camel and a cow, with giraffes and elephants alongside, and the road paints itself in just ahead of the
horse. Geese lift the goat off her ledge on a platform. A turtle stuck in drying mud floats free when two elephants
spray water. A kitten at the bottom of a drain sends up the smallest ring yet, and a chain of otters pulls her out
with a bear's paws holding the top. Then the road goes into dark trees and the paint turns old and cracked: a
smaller version of the same dog tracks along a trail with a warm glow behind him and finds a frightened young bear
behind a log. The bear says one word, colour spreads out from their noses and the cracks heal. A brass lantern
falls into a ditch and goes dark. The dog and the bear walk away together up a road that colours in ahead of them,
into a sunset where the title paints itself over the wagon, which now has a small light on its post. Last, under an
oak, everyone sleeps in a pile with the lantern lit on the wagon; a chipmunk squeaks a thumbnail ring, the dog's ears
shoot up, and the bear answers without opening his eyes.

## Against the premise
It matches. Every call for help, however small, is a ring of paint, and the colour is what the rescue wagon rides on.
The flashback is visible as the old cracked part of the same canvas, and the first fresh colour there comes from the
bear's word. The switch is shown in four frames: the glow behind the dog, the meeting, the lantern in the ditch, the
two leaving together. The same lantern is planted unlit on the wagon in beats 3 and 4, falls in 11, and hangs lit in
13 and 14, so "he kept the light" reads from picture alone. Trouble is on screen before every rescue. No humans
appear anywhere.

## Gaps
Two soft spots, no missing shots.
The viewer will read the flashback dog as "the same dog, younger", but not that the glow behind him is a hunter's light;
the lantern falling in beat 11 is what makes that readable, so it carries more weight than its 3 seconds suggest.
Beat 2 shows the marmot already standing; her sitting-then-rising is sold only by the ring arrival (script note).

# HANK & DOUG'S WILD RESCUE — 60-second trailer, premise v4 ("Doug leaves the hunt")
Written 2026-11-17, writing only. v2's approval is archived as APPROVED_v2.md; there is no APPROVED.md for v4 until
Matt reads this. Design strings, the theme song lyrics and the painted look are unchanged from v2.

## The big idea in two sentences
The trailer tells Doug's origin straight, the way Jeremy tells it: a hunting dog sees the bear he is tracking save a
fox, decides the bear is good, leads his owner's light into a ditch instead, and walks away with the bear. Then it
shows what those two became: the all-animal fire-wagon crew that answers every call, with the song listing the
rescues while each one is on screen.

## What changed from v2/v3 and why (Matt's notes on v3)
- "Needs to be more explanatory": a warm narrator carries the whole first half in short lines (72 words total), so a
  kid who has never heard of the show learns who Doug is, who Hank is, why Doug switched sides, and what they do now.
- "Show him abandoning the hunter": a full beat (row 6) of Doug running the other way with the light chasing him,
  then the lantern tumbling into the ditch on the word "ditch".
- "Show how the bear is saving an animal and that makes him realize the bear is cool": Young Hank frees a fox from a
  fence (row 4), then Doug's reaction and his one line "Huh. That bear is... cool." (row 5).
- "Off the sounds and times": every row of the table names the sound AND the visible action it belongs to, with the
  onset time, and seven rows have a mandatory frame-level sync check in stage 05. The sung verse lands its three
  subjects (goat, turtle, kitten) on the three clips that show them. The chorus line "It's Hank and Doug's Wild
  Rescue!" is sung only when the title is fully painted.
- "Go back and look at our stories": the owner is Jeremy's word and stays off screen (glow + whistle only); the
  ditch, the leaving, the no-humans rule, the wagon team, the rescues are all from the story vault.
- The "sound paints the canvas" device and the one-shot are retired: Matt's notes were about clarity and sync, and
  the ripple opening spent 12 seconds before anyone knew who Doug was.

## Controlling idea
The hunter who switched sides now answers every call for help. The trailer shows the switch and its cause in the
first 33 seconds and what it built in the last 27.

## Canon decisions to flag for Matt and Jeremy
1. **The bear Doug saved is Hank** (carried from v1/v2, approved). Jeremy's canon says "a bear"; naming him Hank is
   our choice and the narrator says it out loud at 0:33.
2. **The bear saving an animal as the cause of Doug's change of heart** is Matt's addition (11/17); the canon gives
   no cause. It fits "had a change of heart" and does not contradict anything.
3. **The fox in the fence** is in the vault (filed June 2026) with a badger or raccoons as the rescuers. Here Young
   Hank frees it, alone, before the crew existed. If Jeremy wants the fox kept for a badger episode, swap the saved
   animal for the turtle in the mud (bear scoops him out) and nothing else moves.
4. **The lantern on the wagon** (v2's "he kept the light") is still on the wagon in every daytime shot but the line
   "Same nose. Same light. Different job." is cut for time; it reads as the wagon's lamp now. Say if you want it back.
5. **"Owner" not "hunter"**: the narrator says "his owner" twice because that is Jeremy's word; Matt said "hunter"
   in his note. Either word works in the slot (same syllable count).

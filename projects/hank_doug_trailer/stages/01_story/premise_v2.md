# HANK & DOUG'S WILD RESCUE — 60-second trailer, premise v2 ("the whoa pass")
Written 2026-11-17, writing only. v1 (premise.md, script.md) stays on disk untouched; Matt approved v1 at 4:09 PM,
that approval is archived as APPROVED_v1.md. v2 keeps every v1 story beat, every design string, the theme song
lyrics and the canon. It changes HOW the minute is shot and adds one object and one visual rule.

## The big idea in two sentences
The whole trailer is one unbroken travelling shot across a single painted canvas, and the canvas is blank linen
until a sound touches it: every call for help (the goat's bleat, the marmot's whistle, a kitten's mew, a chipmunk's
squeak) ripples out as a ring of wet paint, and wherever the ring passes the world gets its colour, so the
rescue wagon is literally riding a road that somebody's voice painted. Doug's flashback is the old, cracked,
colourless part of the same canvas, and the first colour ever to land on it comes from a scared bear saying "Hi."

## Why nobody has seen this
- Painted-look films exist (Loving Vincent, Klaus). A canvas that paints itself in the direction of a SOUND, where
  the brush is the voice of the smallest animal in the frame, is the show's own line ("somebody always hears the
  call") turned into the way the picture is made. The theme is the visual effect; it is not a style laid on top.
- One unbroken shot for a 60-second kids' trailer, with no cut from the ledge to the sunset oak, that passes through
  a flashback without cutting: the past is simply the older, varnish-yellowed, cracked stretch of the same road.
- It is cheap where it is strong. The unpainted state of every shot is derived in code from the finished still
  (canvas weave, pencil lines), the paint ripple and the reveal are code mattes, the "joins" between stills hide
  under motion-blurred whip-pans that ride the sound ripple. One Krea still gives two states; no outpainting, no
  seamless panorama needed; identity cannot drift between the grey and the colour because they are the same pixels.

## The heart: Doug (two layers, both shown, two short lines)
1. **The first call Doug ever heard.** In the flashback the world is old cracked paint, cold blue. Doug tracks,
   the hunter's lantern glow behind him. Young Hank behind the log says "Hi." and warm colour blooms from the two
   noses outward across the cracked canvas, healing it. A kid sees "the bear made it pretty"; a grown-up sees the
   moment a hunting dog's world got its colour. The trailer's opening mechanic (sound paints the world) was setting
   this up the whole time.
2. **He kept the light.** The hunter's brass lantern tumbles into the ditch and goes out (v1 beat). In the button,
   the same dented brass lantern hangs lit on the wagon's front post, the one warm light in the dusk frame, Doug
   asleep under it. It is planted on the wagon in the bench shot and the rollout, so it has been in plain sight all
   along. Doug's V.O. on the walk-away: "Same nose. Same light. Different job." That is the whole series in six words.

## Controlling idea (unchanged)
The hunter who switched sides now answers every call for help, and nobody is too small to make that call. v2 adds
the visual corollary: a call, however small, is what brings the colour into the world.

## Who wants what (unchanged from v1)
Doug wants to make up for the hunting years and cannot hear every call alone; Hank wants an easy day and gives it
up before Doug's ears are even up; the ones in trouble need somebody small to hear them.

## The ending (of the trailer)
The road comes out of the trees into sunset, the title paints itself in with brush strokes on the chorus's last
line, the camera settles on the sleeping pile under the oak with the lantern lit. The chipmunk squeaks: the
smallest ripple of paint in the whole film. Doug's ears shoot up. Hank, eyes shut: "Rolling." Black. Sting.

## World rules that hold in every shot (Jeremy's canon, unchanged)
NO humans, hands, boots or guns anywhere; the owner is only an off-frame lantern glow and a lantern falling into a
ditch. Wooden fire-wagon on four huge spoked wooden wheels, no engine, no tires, pulled by one horse, one zebra,
one camel (one hump), one cow. Two giraffes as ladders, two elephants pump and carry water. Some small animal always
hears the call (marmot, kitten's own mew carried by the ripple, chipmunk). Rescues from the story bank only: goat
airlift by geese, turtle in mud and the elephants, kitten in a drain and the otter chain.

## Canon decisions to flag for Matt and Jeremy
1. **The bear Doug saved is Hank** (carried over from v1, already approved).
2. **New: Doug kept the hunter's lantern; it is the wagon's lamp.** Nothing in Jeremy's canon says what happened to
   the lantern after the ditch. If Jeremy would rather it stay in the ditch, drop the lantern from the WAGON design
   string, cut the six-word V.O., and nothing else moves.
3. **New visual rule: sound paints.** It is a trailer device, not a claim about how the show's world works. If the
   series later wants it, it is already there.

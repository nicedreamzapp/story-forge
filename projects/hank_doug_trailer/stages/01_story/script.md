# HANK & DOUG'S WILD RESCUE — 60-second trailer, script v1
Written 2026-11-17 (stage 01, writing only). 14 shots, 0:00-1:00. Painted look from
`projects/hank_doug_painted/LOOK.md`. Spoken lines: 4 (plus 1 narrator line). On-camera talking shots: 3, 10, 14.

## Rules this script is built on
- Every rescue shows the TROUBLE on screen first, then the signal, then the crew. Nobody yells about a thing
  we have not seen.
- One read at a time, cause then reaction; every shot lists its reads in order and the reads set the length.
- Cast per shot stays small (two or three named designs at most, plus a crowd only when it is the point).
- Each character has ONE design string in the sheet below; every stage after this reuses it word for word
  and never adds adjectives (the "lanky Doug" and "feathered Hank" drifts both came from stray adjectives).
- Flashback shots carry a spoken time marker ("Long time ago").
- NO humans, NO hands, NO boots, NO text in any frame except the title card.
- Pulling shots (4) are staged in clean SIDE PROFILE: team out in front, one wagon, the only vehicle.
- Light sources are named only in the shots that need them (the lantern, shots 9 and 11), never in a style
  string.

## Shot list

### 1 · 0:00-0:04 (4 s) · COLD OPEN, THE TROUBLE
**Picture.** Wide, slightly low angle up a grey painted mountain face at dawn. On a ledge no wider than her
hooves, high up, stands GOAT, all four feet bunched together, head turned down toward us, mouth open. A few
pebbles trickle off the ledge and fall past camera. Sage-green valley far below, soft morning haze.
**Reads.** 1. the height 2. the goat, stuck 3. her bleat (mouth open, pebbles fall)
**Sound.** Wind. One long, wobbly bleat. No music.
**Sentiment.** "Oh no." A held breath.

### 2 · 0:04-0:07 (3 s) · THE SIGNAL
**Picture.** Medium on a sun-warmed boulder in the valley meadow. MARMOT sits hunched, chewing a stem. Her
head snaps up (the bleat has reached her). She rises tall on her hind legs, chest out, and whistles, mouth a
small round O, cheeks puffed. The mountain is a soft blur behind her.
**Reads.** 1. the marmot hears (head snaps up, chewing stops) 2. she stands tall 3. the whistle
**Sound.** The bleat, faint and far. Then one piercing marmot whistle, very loud.
**Sentiment.** "Somebody noticed." A small lift.

### 3 · 0:07-0:11 (4 s) · THE BENCH (talking shot)
**Picture.** Medium two-shot, three-quarter front, on the bench of WAGON parked on a dirt road under oaks,
morning light. DOUG sits on the left, HANK on the right, both faces fully visible. Doug's long ears lift and
swing forward, his head turns toward the far-off whistle. Hank does not look up; his big paw is already
closing around the leather reins. Then Hank's mouth moves once.
**Reads.** 1. Doug's ears lift and swing forward 2. Hank's paw closes on the reins 3. Hank speaks
**Line.** HANK (low, unhurried, almost a yawn): "Rolling."
**Sound.** The marmot whistle tails off. A banjo roll starts under Hank's word and runs into shot 4.
**Sentiment.** A grin: these two have done this a hundred times.

### 4 · 0:11-0:16 (5 s) · ROLLOUT, THE THEME HITS
**Picture.** Wide, clean side profile, camera low beside a dirt road through rolling sage-green hills. From
left to right: TEAM in a single line out in front (horse, zebra, camel, cow, all in brown leather harness),
then WAGON behind them with HANK and DOUG small on the bench, then the two GIRAFFES jogging alongside at the
back on the far side of the road, and the two ELEPHANTS behind the wagon with oak water barrels on their
backs. Dust drifts behind the wheels. A single wagon, the only vehicle on the road.
**Reads.** 1. the team pulling 2. the wagon on huge wooden wheels 3. the giraffes and elephants with it
**Narrator line** (warm, like a storybook read aloud): "Somebody always hears the call."
**Sound.** THEME SONG verse 1 begins full on the first frame. Hooves, wheels, one elephant bellow.
**Sentiment.** Joy. "Here comes the cavalry."
**Note for stage 04.** Wheels and the pulling geometry are LESSONS.json items: any shot where the wheels read
goes to Blender, and must_not forbids loose animals, a second wagon, a rump in the foreground, more heads than
bodies.

### 5 · 0:16-0:20 (4 s) · GOAT, THE RESCUE
**Picture.** Medium on the ledge from shot 1, same grey rock, same dawn light. A flat wooden PLATFORM hangs
on four ropes; a dozen GEESE above it hold the ropes in their beaks, wings beating, and the platform rises
into frame level with the ledge. GOAT steps onto it, front hooves first, then all four. The platform dips a
little under her, then holds.
**Reads.** 1. the platform rising on ropes 2. the geese holding the ropes 3. the goat steps on
**Sound.** Verse 1 continues. Wing beats, one happy short bleat.
**Sentiment.** Wonder. "That's how they do it."

### 6 · 0:20-0:26 (6 s) · TURTLE, TROUBLE THEN RESCUE (one location, reads in sequence)
**Picture.** Medium-wide on a shrunken pond, its edge cracked, drying mud. TURTLE sits sunk to the rim of his
shell in the mud in the center of frame, all four legs paddling in place and going nowhere. Hold. Then two
long grey ELEPHANT trunks swing in from the left and right edges of frame and spray two arcs of water into
the mud around him. The mud darkens, turns to water, rises; the turtle lifts off the bottom and floats,
legs now paddling for real. Only the trunks and the front of each elephant's head enter frame; Hank, Doug
and the wagon are not in this shot.
**Reads.** 1. the turtle stuck, legs paddling in mud 2. the trunks swing in and spray 3. water rises
4. the turtle floats and swims
**Sound.** The CHORUS hits on the first frame ("Hank and Doug!"). Squelch, then two big sprays with the
"SPLOOSH" in the lyric if the cut allows.
**Sentiment.** Worry, then a laugh of relief.

### 7 · 0:26-0:29 (3 s) · KITTEN, THE TROUBLE
**Picture.** Close, looking straight down through an iron storm-drain GRATE set in cobblestones at the edge
of a quiet lane (no people, no vehicles, no signs). Two meters down at the bottom of the stone drain, tiny
KITTEN looks up at us, mouth open in a small mew, a shallow puddle around her paws. The bars of the grate
cross the frame in shadow.
**Reads.** 1. the grate 2. the kitten far below, looking up 3. her mew
**Sound.** Chorus continues. One tiny mew, echoing up the drain.
**Sentiment.** Tenderness. "Get her out."

### 8 · 0:29-0:33 (4 s) · KITTEN, THE OTTER CHAIN
**Picture.** Medium, eye level with the cobblestones. The GRATE lies flipped open beside the hole. Hanging
down into the drain is a chain of four OTTERS, each holding the next one's hind feet with its front paws;
the top otter's hind feet are held by HANK's two big paws at the top of frame (only Hank's forearms and paws
show, dark chocolate-brown fur). The bottom otter comes up out of the hole holding KITTEN gently by the
scruff; the kitten's eyes are wide and her legs dangle.
**Reads.** 1. the otter chain into the hole 2. the kitten lifted out at the bottom of the chain
3. Hank's paws anchoring the top
**Sound.** Chorus continues. Otter chirps, the kitten's small mew, a soft "there you go" rumble (no words).
**Sentiment.** "Yes!"

### 9 · 0:33-0:38 (5 s) · FLASHBACK, THE HUNTER
**Picture.** Night forest in cold silver-blue moonlight. DOUG (same design, a little smaller, no collar yet
or the same collar looking new) moves nose-down along a trail toward camera-left. From off-frame right,
behind him, a warm amber lantern glow spills across the ground and the tree trunks, following him; no
lantern and no one holding it is visible. Doug stops. His head lifts. In front of him, half hidden behind a
fallen log, crouches YOUNG HANK (same design, a little smaller and rounder), eyes wide, ears flat.
**Reads.** 1. Doug tracking, nose down 2. the warm glow following him from off-frame 3. Doug stops
4. the frightened bear behind the log
**Line.** DOUG (V.O., quiet, older, telling it): "Long time ago... I hunted bears."
**Sound.** The song drops to the music-box breakdown. Night crickets. Doug's sniffing.
**Sentiment.** Quiet. "Wait, Doug was a hunter?"
**Note.** The glow is a prop for THIS shot only; it is not in any style string. must_not: a visible lantern,
a hand, a person, a boot, a gun.

### 10 · 0:38-0:42 (4 s) · NOSE TO NOSE (talking shot)
**Picture.** Close two-shot in moonlight, side-on. DOUG's face on the left, YOUNG HANK's face on the right,
noses almost touching over the top of the log. The bear's eyes are wide and scared. Then his mouth moves,
one small word. Doug's eyes soften; behind him his tail starts to wag (the tail is the last read).
**Reads.** 1. the two faces nose to nose 2. the bear speaks 3. Doug's tail wags
**Line.** DOUG (V.O., finishing the thought, before the bear speaks): "Then I met one."
**Line.** YOUNG HANK (small, hopeful, on camera): "Hi."
**Sound.** Music box only. A long beat of crickets before "Hi."
**Sentiment.** Surprise, then a soft laugh.

### 11 · 0:42-0:45 (3 s) · THE DITCH
**Picture.** Low wide along the forest edge at night. In the foreground a muddy roadside DITCH. A brass
LANTERN, alone, tumbles end over end into frame from above-right, lands in the mud with a splash, hisses
and goes dark. Nothing and no one else is in the frame; the moonlit road runs away into the background.
**Reads.** 1. the lantern falls 2. the splash 3. the light goes out (frame goes to moonlight only)
**Sound.** Music box. A clank, a splash, a hiss. Then just crickets.
**Sentiment.** "He chose the bear." A little thrill.
**Note.** must_not: a person, a hand, an arm, a boot, a second lantern.

### 12 · 0:45-0:48 (3 s) · WALKING AWAY TOGETHER
**Picture.** Wide from behind, the moonlit dirt road running into soft silver hills. DOUG and YOUNG HANK
walk away from camera side by side, the bear's shoulder a head taller than the dog, Doug's tail up and
swinging. They are the only figures in the frame.
**Reads.** 1. the two of them walking away together 2. Doug's tail swinging
**Sound.** Chorus 2 builds from "Nobody's too big..."
**Sentiment.** Warmth.

### 13 · 0:48-0:53 (5 s) · TITLE CARD
**Picture.** Painted title on a warm ochre-and-rust sunset sky over sage-green hills, the WAGON a small
dark silhouette on a hilltop with the team and the two giraffes in profile. Text, hand-lettered storybook
style, two lines: "HANK & DOUG'S" over "WILD RESCUE". The only text in the trailer.
**Reads.** 1. the silhouette 2. the title (25 characters: minimum 25/15 + 1.5 = 3.2 s fully visible; 5 s given)
**Sound.** "It's Hank and Doug's Wild Rescue!" lands on the first frame of the title. Final chord, then cut.
**Sentiment.** Pride. Singing along.

### 14 · 0:53-1:00 (7 s) · BUTTON (talking shot)
**Picture.** Medium-wide at golden sunset under a big oak: WAGON parked, everyone asleep in a pile. HANK
slumped on the bench, head back, mouth slightly open. DOUG asleep sprawled across Hank's lap, ears hanging
to the floorboards. One GIRAFFE neck droops over the wagon from behind, asleep. MARMOT curled on top of
Hank's head. Hold on the quiet. Then CHIPMUNK pops up onto the rim of the near wheel in the foreground and
squeaks. Doug's ears shoot straight up and his eyes open wide. Hank's eyes stay shut; only his mouth moves.
**Reads.** 1. the sleeping pile 2. the chipmunk pops up and squeaks 3. Doug's ears shoot up
4. Hank speaks without opening his eyes
**Line.** HANK (eyes closed, flat, immediate): "Rolling."
**Sound.** Silence after the title chord. One sharp chipmunk squeak. A beat. Hank's word, then the 2-second
sting "Rollin'! Here we go!" and the cut to black.
**Sentiment.** A laugh. "I want more."

## Story test (from the shot list ALONE, no narration, no lines, no song)
A white goat is stuck on a tiny ledge high up a mountain, pebbles falling. Down in the meadow a marmot hears
her, stands up and whistles. On a parked wooden wagon a bloodhound's ears lift toward the sound and the big
bear next to him already has the reins in his paw. The wagon rolls out on huge wooden wheels pulled by a
horse, a zebra, a camel and a cow, with two giraffes and two elephants running along. A platform carried by
geese rises to the goat's ledge and she steps on. A turtle is sunk in drying mud; two elephant trunks spray
water in and he floats free. A kitten is at the bottom of a storm drain; a chain of otters hangs down into it,
the bear holding the top, and the kitten comes up. At night, in the past, the same dog is tracking with a
lantern glow following behind him, and finds a scared bear behind a log. They touch noses; the dog's tail
wags. A lantern falls into a ditch and goes out. The dog and the bear walk off down the moonlit road
together. Title. At sunset the whole crew sleeps in a pile; a chipmunk squeaks; the dog's ears shoot up and
the bear answers without opening his eyes.

**Against the premise** ("the hunter who switched sides now answers every call, and nobody is too small to
make that call"): the switch is SHOWN (tracking, the glow following, the meeting, the glow dumped in the
ditch, leaving together), the words only confirm it. "Nobody too small" is shown twice (marmot, chipmunk).
Every rescue shows the trouble before the signal or the crew. No humans anywhere. Holds.

**Gaps checked.** The earlier film never showed the animal in trouble before the yell: here shots 1, 6 and 7
are pure trouble shots. Bear fur: the Hank string says "soft dense brush marks... no feather shapes, no wings".
Color drift: every design string carries a color lock and no shot may add color words.

# HANK & DOUG'S WILD RESCUE — 60-second trailer (painted look), premise
Written 2026-11-17. Stage 01 only: no render, no stills, no `.sf`.

## Who it is for
Kids about 4 to 9 and the grown-ups watching with them. The trailer has to sell the SHOW in one minute: who
these two are, what they do, who rides with them, and why Doug is the way he is.

## The problem the last cut had (Matt stopped it)
The story was unclear: an animal yelled about trouble the viewer had never seen. Hank's fur read as feathers.
A character changed color between shots. This trailer is built against those three failures: every rescue shows
the trouble on screen BEFORE anyone signals; every character has one fixed design string reused word for word
(script.md, "Character design sheet"); the cast in each shot is kept small so identity holds.

## Controlling idea
The hunter who switched sides now answers every call for help, and nobody is too small to make that call.

## Who wants what, what stops them
- **Doug** (bloodhound, ex bear-hunter) wants to make up for the hunting years by finding whoever is lost or
  stuck. What stops him: he cannot hear every call himself. He needs the whole animal world to pass it along.
- **Hank** (bear) wants an easy day, and gives it up every time. What stops him: nothing, which is the joke.
  Before Doug has finished lifting his ears, Hank is already reaching for the reins.
- **The ones in trouble** (a goat on a ledge, a turtle in drying mud, a kitten down a drain) want out, and
  cannot do it alone. What saves them: some small animal hears, signals, and the wagon comes.

## The ending (of the trailer)
The theme song's chorus lands on the title card. Then the button: the whole crew asleep in a pile at sunset, a
chipmunk squeaks a call, Doug's ears shoot up, and Hank, eyes still closed, says "Rolling." The last line of
the trailer is the first line of every episode.

## The one thing to remember
Somebody always hears the call, and Hank and Doug always come.

## World rules that hold in every shot (Jeremy's canon)
- NO humans anywhere, not even a hand or a boot. Doug's old owner exists only as a lantern glow from off-frame
  and a lantern falling into a ditch.
- The fire-WAGON is a big wooden wagon on four huge spoked wooden wheels: never a truck, no tires, no engine.
  Pulled by a team of four different animals: one horse, one zebra, one camel, one cow (Matt 11/1, overrides
  "horses, camels and cows").
- Two giraffes ride along as ladders. Two elephants pump and carry water.
- Some animal always hears the call and signals the crew; a different animal each time. In this trailer: a
  marmot on a boulder (cold open) and a chipmunk (button).
- Rescue ideas come from the story bank only: nanny goat high on a mountain saved by a bird-powered airlift
  platform; turtle stuck in mud while the elephants fill the pond; kitten in a storm drain pulled out by an
  otter chain.

## Doug's backstory, told gently (kid-safe)
Spoken time marker required for the flashback ("Long time ago..."). Shown: Doug tracking at night with a warm
lantern glow following him from off-frame; a frightened bear hiding behind a log; the two of them nose to nose;
Doug turns and leads the glow the OTHER way; the lantern alone tumbles into a muddy ditch and goes out; Doug
and the bear walk off down the moonlit road together. No chase, no weapon, no fall, no one hurt.

## Canon decision made here (flag for Matt)
The bear Doug saved IS Hank, a little smaller and rounder, same design string. Jeremy's canon says Doug "led his
owner into a ditch so he could save a bear, and then left to rescue animals alongside Hank"; the earlier
trailer kept the bear anonymous. Making it Hank gives the one-minute trailer a single, warm cause-and-effect
a kid can follow, and lets the bear's one word in the flashback ("Hi.") pay off the friendship we have just
watched for forty seconds. If Matt wants the bear kept anonymous, change the shot-10 design line to a lighter
cinnamon-brown bear and drop the word "Hi."; nothing else moves.

## Sentiment per beat (what the viewer should feel)

| Beat | What is on screen | Sentiment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cold open | a goat on a tiny ledge, pebbles falling | "oh no", a held breath |
| 2 the signal | a marmot hears it and whistles | "somebody noticed", a small lift |
| 3 the bench | Doug's ears, Hank's paw already on the reins, "Rolling." | a grin: these two have done this before |
| 4 rollout | the wagon and the whole crew, theme song hits | joy, "here comes the cavalry" |
| 5 goat airlift | geese lift a platform to the ledge | wonder, "that's how they do it" |
| 6 turtle | sunk in mud, then elephants spray, then floating | worry, then a laugh of relief |
| 7-8 kitten | down a drain, then an otter chain hauls it up | tenderness, then "yes!" |
| 9 flashback | night, Doug tracking, a scared bear behind a log | quiet, "wait, Doug was a hunter?" |
| 10 nose to nose | the bear says "Hi." | surprise, then a soft laugh |
| 11 the ditch | the lantern falls in and goes out | "he chose the bear", a little thrill |
| 12 walk away | Doug and the bear leave together | warmth |
| 13 title | HANK & DOUG'S WILD RESCUE, chorus lands | pride, singing along |
| 14 button | the sleeping pile, a chipmunk squeak, "Rolling." | a laugh, "I want more" |

ANCHOR: Water has been rising out of the ground since early this morning. At the current rate, Silver Springs will be under 400 feet of water by 3 PM tomorrow.

(splashes) The Lahontan Reservoir behind me is overflowing in the wrong direction, uphill. Locals say the sagebrush started floating around noon.
DR. HALVERSON (Desert Hydrology Institute; older man, glasses, scribbled whiteboard): We always knew the old shorelines up on those hills were a warning. You can still see the bathtub rings. The lake was just... waiting. Honestly, it looks like it wants its valley back.
RESIDENT (grizzled man, lawn chair, already in a life jacket, Nevada drawl): I been here 30 years. Never had beachfront property. Now I'm gonna have underwater property. Already bought a pontoon off a guy in Fallon.
ANCHOR: Officials are urging residents to get ready now. Move valuables to the second floor, or better, the roof. Fill your boats with gas. Bring your pets inside, unless they're fish. Fernley and Dayton should expect waterfront property by Sunday.
GRAPHIC: "EVACUATION ROUTE — UP"
ANCHOR: If you see a prehistoric fish swimming down your street, do not approach it. We'll stay on this story as it develops. For Silver Springs, I'm Dan Rourke. Stay dry, Nevada.
